05 — HISTORY

Follow the timeline

Music changes alongside technology, politics, economics, migration, social movements, and culture. Follow some of the major developments that shaped recorded music.

1877–1920s

The beginning of recorded sound

Mechanical recording technologies made it possible to preserve and reproduce performances. Music increasingly became something that could be owned, distributed, and listened to outside a live setting.

1920s–1940s

Radio, jazz, and mass audiences

Radio transformed how people encountered music. Jazz developed rapidly as musicians experimented with rhythm, improvisation, harmony, and ensemble interaction.

1950s–1970s

Rock, soul, and popular music

Rock and roll, soul, Motown, folk, funk, and other movements reshaped popular music. Music also became increasingly connected to youth culture and social change.

1970s–1990s

Hip-hop, sampling, and new production

Hip-hop emerged from Black and Latino communities in New York and developed new approaches to rhythm, DJing, sampling, MCing, and production that would influence global popular music.

1980s–2000s

Digital music

Digital recording, CDs, synthesizers, computers, and eventually file-sharing changed how music was produced, distributed, purchased, and experienced.

2000s–present

Streaming and the global music ecosystem

Streaming platforms have made enormous catalogs immediately accessible while changing how artists release music, how listeners discover it, and how the music industry earns revenue.

06 — GENRES

Explore genres

Genres are useful starting points, but they overlap, evolve, and often reflect cultural histories that are more complicated than a single label suggests.

UNITED STATES

Rock

A broad family of styles rooted in earlier forms of popular music, blues, rhythm and blues, and country.

AFRICAN AMERICAN TRADITIONS

Jazz

Characterized by improvisation, rhythmic complexity, distinctive harmonic approaches, and a history closely connected to Black American culture.

GLOBAL POPULAR MUSIC

Pop

A broad category of commercially distributed popular music shaped by changing technologies, trends, and audience tastes.

BRONX · NEW YORK

Hip-Hop

A cultural movement and musical tradition involving MCing, DJing, breakdancing, graffiti, sampling, production, and diverse forms of rap.

UNITED STATES

R&B

A broad tradition that developed from rhythm and blues and became connected to soul, funk, pop, hip-hop, and contemporary production.

EUROPEAN TRADITIONS

Classical

A broad category encompassing centuries of composed Western art music, from early traditions through modern and contemporary works.

GLOBAL TRADITIONS

Folk

Music connected to particular communities, traditions, stories, practices, and forms of cultural transmission.

20TH–21ST CENTURY

Electronic

Music created or substantially shaped through electronic instruments, digital production, synthesis, sampling, and computer technology.

UNITED STATES

Country

A diverse American tradition influenced by folk, blues, gospel, and other musical traditions, often centered on storytelling and songcraft.
