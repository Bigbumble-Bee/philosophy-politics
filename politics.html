Government

WHO HAS POWER?

Governments differ in how political authority is distributed, how leaders are selected, and how citizens participate.

Democracy

Political authority is connected to the participation and consent of the people. Democratic systems can take many forms, including representative and direct democracy.

Monarchy

A system in which a monarch occupies the head of state. Monarchies can range from constitutional systems to systems where monarchs exercise substantial political authority.

Republic

A political system in which government is understood as a public matter and political authority is exercised through institutions rather than hereditary monarchy.

Authoritarianism

A political system characterized by concentrated political power and limited political competition or participation.

Federalism

A system in which constitutional authority is divided between a national government and regional governments.

Separation of Powers

The distribution of government functions among institutions so that political power is not concentrated in one place.

Political timeline

IDEAS → INSTITUTIONS → MOVEMENTS
Ancient World

Early political thought

Greek and Roman thinkers developed systematic ideas about citizenship, justice, law, constitutions, republican government, and the responsibilities of political leaders.

Medieval Period

Authority, law, and political order

Political thought developed around questions of divine authority, natural law, monarchy, political obligation, religious institutions, and the relationship between spiritual and temporal power.

1500s–1600s

The modern state emerges

Thinkers such as Machiavelli, Hobbes, and Locke reconsidered sovereignty, political authority, security, rights, and the relationship between individuals and government.

1700s

The Enlightenment

Ideas about reason, natural rights, consent, liberty, religious toleration, constitutionalism, and popular sovereignty became increasingly influential.

1776–1789

Revolutionary political thought

The American and French Revolutions became major historical experiments involving rights, citizenship, sovereignty, constitutional government, and political equality.

1800s

Industrialization and mass politics

Industrial capitalism, nationalism, socialism, labor movements, expanded suffrage, and new ideas about class transformed political debate.

1900s–Present

Mass democracy and global politics

Political thought expanded around democracy, totalitarianism, decolonization, civil rights, feminism, globalization, human rights, political economy, and contemporary democratic theory.

Political vocabulary

WORDS WORTH KNOWING

Sovereignty

Supreme political authority within a particular territory or political community.

Legitimacy

The quality of being regarded as having rightful or justified political authority.

Constitutionalism

The principle that government authority is structured and limited by constitutional rules.

Political Obligation

The question of whether and why citizens have a moral duty to obey political authority.

Pluralism

The idea that political power and social interests are distributed among multiple groups.

Populism

A political style or ideology that commonly frames politics as a struggle between ordinary people and an elite.

Questions worth investigating

DON'T JUST CONSUME POLITICS

What makes a government legitimate?

Compare consent, democracy, natural rights, security, tradition, and other theories of political authority.

How much power should government have?

Investigate liberty, security, public goods, individual rights, and the problem of concentrated power.

What does political equality actually mean?

Consider equal rights, equal political participation, equal opportunity, and economic equality.

Can democracy protect itself from becoming undemocratic?

Investigate constitutional limits, institutions, political norms, and the tension between majority rule and minority rights.

When is political protest justified?

Explore civil disobedience, political obligation, resistance, law, and legitimacy.

Does economic inequality threaten political equality?

Compare competing theories of wealth, power, property, opportunity, and political influence.



Research framework

Use this structure whenever you investigate a political issue. Separate evidence from interpretation and arguments from conclusions.

01 — Question What exactly are you trying to find out?
02 — Definitions What do the important terms actually mean?
03 — Evidence What reliable evidence exists?
04 — Arguments What are the strongest competing explanations?
05 — Assumptions What does each position take for granted?
06 — Conclusion What does the evidence justify believing?
