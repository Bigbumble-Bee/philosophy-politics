01 — HISTORY

A timeline of thought

Philosophy changes as people ask new questions, challenge old assumptions, and reinterpret earlier ideas.

c. 600–300 BCE

Ancient Greek Philosophy

Early philosophers began investigating nature, knowledge, virtue, politics, and the nature of reality through reason and argument.

Thales · Socrates · Plato · Aristotle
c. 300 BCE–300 CE

Hellenistic Philosophy

Philosophical schools focused heavily on how people should live, respond to suffering, pursue happiness, and understand their place in the world.

Stoicism · Epicureanism · Skepticism
c. 400–1400

Medieval Philosophy

Philosophers explored relationships between reason, faith, morality, metaphysics, and knowledge.

Augustine · Avicenna · Aquinas
c. 1400–1700

Renaissance & Early Modern Thought

Humanism, scientific discovery, political change, and new approaches to knowledge transformed European philosophy.

Machiavelli · Descartes · Hobbes · Locke
c. 1700–1800

The Enlightenment

Philosophers examined reason, liberty, government, individual rights, morality, and the limits of human knowledge.

Hume · Rousseau · Kant
1800–1900s

Modern Philosophy

Philosophers challenged traditional ideas about history, morality, society, freedom, existence, and human nature.

Hegel · Marx · Kierkegaard · Nietzsche
1900s–Present

Contemporary Philosophy

Philosophy expanded into language, mind, science, politics, ethics, artificial intelligence, identity, and many other areas of human life.

Analytic · Continental · Pragmatism · Existentialism

02 — THINKERS

Philosophers to know

Start with influential thinkers, then follow their ideas into the larger philosophical traditions they helped shape.

ANCIENT GREECE

Socrates

Known for questioning assumptions and examining concepts such as virtue, knowledge, and justice.

ANCIENT GREECE

Plato

Explored reality, knowledge, justice, education, politics, and the relationship between appearance and truth.

ANCIENT GREECE

Aristotle

Developed influential work in logic, ethics, politics, metaphysics, biology, and the study of knowledge.

EARLY MODERN

René Descartes

Investigated certainty, doubt, reason, and the relationship between mind and body.

EARLY MODERN

John Locke

Developed influential ideas about knowledge, natural rights, government, and individual political liberty.

ENLIGHTENMENT

Immanuel Kant

Examined reason, morality, knowledge, freedom, and the conditions that shape human experience.

19TH CENTURY

Friedrich Nietzsche

Critiqued traditional morality, examined power and values, and explored questions about meaning and human existence.

20TH CENTURY

Simone de Beauvoir

Explored freedom, ethics, social structures, gender, and the relationship between individual existence and society.

03 — TRADITIONS

Schools of thought

Different philosophical traditions approach questions from different starting points.

01

Stoicism

Focuses on virtue, rational judgment, and distinguishing what is within our control from what is not.

02

Rationalism

Emphasizes reason as an important source of knowledge and understanding.

03

Empiricism

Emphasizes observation and experience in the development of knowledge.

04

Existentialism

Investigates freedom, responsibility, meaning, choice, and human existence.

05

Pragmatism

Examines ideas partly through their practical consequences and usefulness.

06

Utilitarianism

Evaluates actions partly according to their consequences for overall well-being.

04 — VOCABULARY

Concepts to know

Philosophy becomes easier to navigate once its recurring vocabulary becomes familiar.

Epistemology

The study of knowledge, belief, justification, and how we know what we know.

Metaphysics

The study of reality, existence, being, causation, and what fundamentally exists.

Ethics

The philosophical study of morality, right action, wrong action, and how we ought to live.

Logic

The study of valid reasoning and the structure of arguments.

Ontology

The study of being and the categories of things that exist.

Aesthetics

The philosophical study of beauty, art, taste, and aesthetic experience.

Political Philosophy

The study of justice, authority, rights, government, liberty, and political society.

Philosophy of Mind

The study of consciousness, thought, perception, identity, and the relationship between mind and body.

05 — THE QUESTIONS

Philosophy begins with a question.

What makes knowledge reliable?

What makes an action morally right?

Do humans have free will?

What makes a government legitimate?

What is the relationship between the individual and society?

Can we ever know reality as it truly is?
